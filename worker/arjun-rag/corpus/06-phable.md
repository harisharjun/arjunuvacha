# Phable Care — Senior Product Manager (Jun 2022 – Jan 2023)

## The retention problem and field research
Phable offered a SaaS platform for clinics for chronic disease management. Despite 12,000 doctors onboarded, week-over-week doctor/clinic retention was ~10%. Arjun's mandate: find root causes and fix retention. He visited 40+ clinics across Bengaluru, Chennai, and Hyderabad doing primary field research — interviewing doctors and staff and mapping the end-to-end patient journey. Key insights: 80–85% of patients were walk-ins with no appointments, suffering unpredictable 15-minute-to-2-hour waits; staff ran operations on paper registers (error-prone, no status tracking); doctors had zero visibility into daily volumes, fees collected, or new-vs-follow-up splits. The existing product only did week-ahead appointment booking — useless for walk-in reality.

## The queue management system (retention 10% → 65%)
Arjun catalogued 25–28 pain points, scored them by frequency × severity, and focused phase 1 on replacing the physical register. The MVP: a single-page real-time Patient Queue Management System with automatic token assignment, live status transitions (waiting → consulting → completed → paid), day-level views instead of weekly calendars, quick walk-in booking, and basic payment tracking. He validated low-fi and hi-fi Figma prototypes directly with doctors (3–4 UI iterations before handoff), built a training module so sales trained clinic staff rather than just pitching doctors, ran a 3-week controlled pilot with 40 clinics (personally onboarding the first 7–10), then rolled out to 300 clinics. Week-1 clinic/doctor retention rose from 10% to 65% w-o-w.

## Influencing the engineering lead (conviction without authority)
The engineering manager initially pushed back on the queue system — it required architectural changes he felt weren't justified. Arjun walked him through the 40-clinic research with anecdotes and data (one doctor: "with the calendar I go to three places to manage a patient; with the queue it's one"). When he still hesitated, Arjun shifted the conversation from "is this worth the architectural effort" to "what's our plan to fix doctor retention this year" — the company goal the lead was accountable for — and brought him into scoping the MVP rather than handing him a spec. His stated lesson: influence isn't having the stronger argument; it's making your ask land in terms of what the other person is accountable for.

## Payments product and program delivery conflict
Arjun also built an intuitive internal payments product for the Ops team, cutting their turnaround time from 66 seconds to 28 seconds per transaction. Separately, tasked with digitising health-program delivery (12/24-week programs run on spreadsheets) within a month of joining, he hit a cold, unresponsive program lead. Instead of escalating a complaint, he did the homework himself — talking to delivery managers, studying the spreadsheets and customer communications, interviewing customers — then presented a findings brief and draft proposal to the CPO and convened a joint meeting. Work moved, and a three-sprint roadmap emerged. His takeaway: the initial friction was partly his own lack of homework; own your preparation before depending on others.
