# Darwinbox — Product Manager II, Helpdesk module (Apr 2025 – present)

## Role and scope
Arjun owns the Helpdesk module end-to-end at Darwinbox, a global HR-tech enterprise SaaS company with 1,400+ global clients. Helpdesk is an AI-powered creation and resolution platform adopted by ~60% of those 1,400+ clients, including large enterprises with 50,000+ employees (that number describes the size of some client organisations, not a total user count), with three distinct personas (ticket creators/employees, agents, admins) and 100+ configurations controlling its pages. He leads a cross-functional pod (engineering, design, QA, client success) that has delivered 100% on-time, zero-defect releases, balancing CPO/CTO direction, enterprise client needs, and engineering velocity.

## The AI workflows (40% agent time saved)
Arjun designed and shipped Helpdesk's AI-powered creation workflows: the AI Assistant auto-drafts contextual responses for agents, generates structured diagnoses, and surfaces resolution suggestions; the Super Agent resolves employee queries autonomously before they ever become tickets, escalating to a human only with a full AI-synthesised summary (a human-in-the-loop, or HITL, escalation model: autonomous first-line resolution, human intervention only when needed). Together, deflection and agent assistance are validated to save 40% of human agent time. Adoption is tracked continuously across clients as the core iteration metric.

## The knowledge graph and eval framework behind the AI
The trust layer that makes the AI usable: Arjun designed Helpdesk's knowledge/context graph — 20 entities spanning the service catalog, routing logic, SLA contracts, access tiers, and context retrieval — as the shared data layer every AI output is grounded in, turning generic LLM answers into context-aware, sendable ones. Separately, he built a suite of 100+ Evals as the pre-launch pass/fail quality bar for the AI features — covering resolution accuracy, failure modes, and SLA compliance — before anything shipped. He also ran an AI development POC in which structured, PM-authored PRDs directly generated functional end-to-end code — compressing the gap between product definition and working software.

## The 11-city listening tour and UX revamp
Arjun ran the module's largest-ever client engagement: 11 city visits surfacing 100+ product gaps, managing conflicting priorities across 39 client organisations of varying size, industry, and HR maturity. For the org-wide UX revamp he assessed whether Helpdesk needed cosmetic or functional change using a three-step method: map user journeys and JTBDs per persona, rank JTBDs, then test whether the existing UI supported them with data. Data insights that changed the design: ~30% of admins actually behaved as dual-role agents (so a unified interface was needed); 95% of agent time was on 2 of 7 pages (the other 5 were an information-discovery failure); acting on about-to-breach tickets — a top JTBD — had no UI support while clients saw 10% of tickets breach; high-utility features (template responses used by 36% of clients, closure notes on 76% of closed tickets, CC on 13% of tickets) were buried while sub-1%-usage features had prominence. Results: monthly production issues fell from 18 to under 3 across 1,000+ clients; in the first 3 months post-revamp, breach rate was down from 10% to 7% and average resolution time down from 70 to 58 hours.

## The non-employee ticketing conflict (CTO story)
Four clients asked to let non-employees raise tickets; the CTO rejected it — "our Helpdesk is for employees." Instead of dropping it, Arjun interviewed 14 clients and found every one handled ex-employee background-verification (BGV) requests through offline, non-auditable email threads landing in HRBP mailboxes. He reframed the persona from "external stranger" to "ex-employee" and proposed: BGV agencies email a dedicated ID, a ticket auto-creates and routes to the right HRBP, everything auditable end-to-end. The CTO engaged but raised spam and pricing concerns — the spam concern was one Arjun admits he had missed. He came back with whitelisting/blacklisting and per-email/hour/client rate limits, plus a credit-based pricing model (1 credit per email received) since per-employee-per-month pricing didn't fit. The CTO approved; the feature became one of Helpdesk's most used and opened a new credits revenue channel. Arjun tells this as his conflict story: take the objection seriously, do the field work, and come back with the nuance that changes the frame.
