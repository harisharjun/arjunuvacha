# Darwinbox — Employee Relations module (0→1 discovery and greenlight)

## Finding the whitespace
Darwinbox's service-delivery SKU had stagnant ARR. Nobody was tasked with fixing it — Arjun took it on himself, exploring adjacent opportunities beyond Helpdesk. He identified Employee Relations (ER) — grievances, disciplinary processes, workplace investigations — as a market with only 3–4 focused players (HR Acuity, Dovetail, AllVoices, plus ServiceNow adjacency), worth roughly $5B and expected to double by 2035. Clients were stitching ER together with spreadsheets and email. It was in no PM's roadmap; he claimed the gap.

## Validation and the vibe-coded prototype
Arjun started with primary research with 3 enterprise clients in India that confirmed the service-delivery gap, then ran a structured validation across 11 client interviews and a full competitive teardown of HR Acuity, Dovetail, AllVoices, and ServiceNow. Instead of pitching with slides, he vibe-coded a working prototype end-to-end using AI tools, so stakeholders could click through the product rather than imagine it. He presented the research, business case (market analysis, competitive benchmarking, sizing), and prototype — winning CPO alignment, then navigating sales and finance approval to formally greenlight the new product line.

## Product definition and status
Arjun drove the product definition: a data-isolated case management module with investigation workflows and strict access restrictions — deliberately descoping the anonymity use case to cut release timelines from 4 months to 2. He coordinated marketing and prepared the sales motion for the phase-1 launch. Current status: engineering build in progress, with a market opportunity of $500K ARR potential. He tells this as his ownership story: no mandate, no team, no budget — conviction built through research and a prototype he made himself.
