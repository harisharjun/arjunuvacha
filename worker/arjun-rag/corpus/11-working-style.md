# Working style, leadership, and values

## How Arjun works with engineering and design
Arjun treats engineers as partners, not code-writers. His operating system: be customer-centric with clear business goals; build rapport and a common goal; align teams with open vision/strategy/roadmap documents and a biweekly cadence; empower teams by showing user impact, giving ownership of decisions, and crediting every milestone; document everything. He summarises it as SAEED: Start with the user's problem, Align on vision and strategy, set Expectations through regular cadences, Empower teams to solve problems, Document everything — plus resolving conflicts through facilitation and rapport. Evidence: his Darwinbox pod's 100% on-time, zero-defect release record.

## How Arjun influences without authority
His influence pattern, proven at Phable (engineering lead) and Darwinbox (CTO): do the field research first, bring anecdotes and data rather than opinions, and reframe the ask in terms of what the other person is accountable for — a company goal, a persona they hadn't considered, a risk they care about. When stakeholders raise objections, he treats them as design input (the CTO's spam concern became rate-limiting and whitelisting features) rather than resistance. He involves skeptics in scoping so their objection becomes their problem to solve too.

## Strengths
What Arjun is best at: finding the real problem behind the stated one (uncertainty, not features, drove Phable churn; ex-employee BGV, not "external tickets," was the real client need); building conviction without authority; prototyping with his own hands; designing workflows for non-technical users (construction field workers, clinic staff, HR admins); and pairing every product case with a business case — a habit from his sales years.

## Weaknesses and failure handling
Arjun is candid about failure. WhatsDplan taught him he had confirmation-biased research and over-built before validating. The Phable program-lead friction taught him to do his homework before depending on others for knowledge transfer. The CTO spam objection taught him he can dismiss risks he hasn't personally felt. His pattern in all three: name the mistake specifically, extract a reusable rule, and show the rule changing later behavior.

## Values and personality
Arjun's blog reveals the person: essays on cognitive biases (confirmation bias, IKEA effect, framing, hyperbolic discounting), book notes (Hooked, The 7 Habits, The Heartfulness Way), the art of saying no, the value of defaults, and gratitude lists of things that made him smile. The blog's name, Arjun Uvacha ("Arjun said"), reflects how he sees himself: the questioner in the dialogue, always asking. He believes setbacks are opportunities to reset — and wrote exactly that essay after living it.
