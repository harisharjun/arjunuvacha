# Cuemath — Product Manager, Growth pod (Mar 2021 – Jun 2022)

## Demo-to-enrollment conversion (18% → 29%)
At Cuemath (K-8 math ed-tech), Arjun led the customer acquisition growth pod focused on the bottom of the funnel: demo-completed → paid enrollment. He interviewed 35-50+ parents and uncovered the real friction: standard demos didn't diagnose each child's specific disposition (math anxiety, Olympiad focus, visual learning needs); explicit trust in the teacher was the deal-breaker; parents were caught off guard by structural needs (dedicated laptop) and generic sales counselling; and final decisions needed both parents while only one attended the demo. His three-phase roadmap: (1) pre-demo personalization — an ingestion engine capturing parent preferences and child problem areas, surfaced on a teacher dashboard so instructors could hyper-target the live demo; (2) post-demo engagement — a parent dashboard with child certificates, diagnostic reports, math puzzles, and teacher credentials; (3) automated predictive lead scoring routed into the counsellor platform. Result: demo-to-enrollment conversion rose from 18% to 29% and TAT dropped 30% (2.9 → 2.02 days).

## What parents actually asked, wanted, and feared
From Arjun's 35 parent interviews at Cuemath — questions: "Will this help my child's specific needs?", "What are the teacher's qualifications?", "Does the curriculum match my child's school?", "Do I need a laptop?". Motivations: tuition beyond school, math as a career foundation, better use of spare time than video games, building logical and problem-solving skills. Barriers: unaddressed queries, high annual fees, fear of committing before knowing the program's worth, and whether the child would like the teacher. Each phase of his roadmap mapped directly to one of these frictions — pre-demo inputs to customization gaps, teacher dashboards and credentials to trust, post-demo reports and lead scoring to the two-parent decision problem.

## Growth experiments
Brainly channel — hypothesis: math learners on Brainly would enroll with Cuemath. Setup: a Cuemath landing page on Brainly and a co-branded sign-up flow, exposed to 50% of North American math learners. Result: 15–20 leads/day but ~60% junk rate, weak program interest, and registration-to-demo and demo-to-payment conversion far below other channels. Shut down; lesson: channels without upfront qualification just relocate cost to sales. Freemium portal — hypothesis: free self-serve access would improve and shorten conversion. A 4-week experiment routed organic blog traffic to a freemium K-8 learning system: sign-up conversion rose 5.2% → 9.9%, logins 19% → 60%, 300+ registrations — but week-1 retention was 20% and demo bookings under 1% (vs 30% in the regular flow). 30+ parent conversations explained it: parents wanted live human instruction, lacked time or confidence to guide the child through software, and saw it as practice rather than learning. A 300-parent pilot confirmed the custom-tutor preference; the freemium model was scrapped. Arjun cites both as evidence he kills his own experiments when the value metrics (not the vanity metrics) say no.

## Payments
Arjun studied the PayPal Smart Payment Buttons API documentation himself and integrated Pay Later into Cuemath's payments module, increasing payment conversion for parents by lowering the upfront barrier.
