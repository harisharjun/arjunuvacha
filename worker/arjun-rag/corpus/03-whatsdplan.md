# WhatsDplan — startup co-founder (Mar 2015 – Mar 2017)

## The startup
Arjun co-founded WhatsDplan in Hyderabad with a co-founder from NIT Warangal — a B2C consumer app to help groups of friends discover places and events, create hangout plans, and decide together. As product and growth lead he did user research, wireframes, workflows, design, beta testing, GTM strategy, and process design; he also built the WhatsDplan website himself using Bootstrap, HTML5, JS, and CSS3, and handled marketing, finances, and business development. Results: 5,000+ Play Store installs, 100+ MAUs, ₹0 → ₹50K MRR, and MoUs signed with Zomato, EventsHigh, play zones, drive-ins, and restaurants for affiliate marketing.

## Three iterations and a pivot
Iteration 1 (planner app): 6–7 months building a detailed app letting friend groups vote on event times and places; retention collapsed to near zero within weeks — changing group coordination habits away from WhatsApp proved brutally hard. Iteration 2 (events and listings): partnered with NIT Warangal's tech fest as an event guide (good brief traction), then expanded into localized listings using Zomato and EventsHigh APIs; retention stayed low. Iteration 3 (group discount discovery): pivoted to targeted group deals (e.g., 20% off for groups of 4) with local restaurants, bars, and play zones — this finally created repeat behavior, but the model needed heavy localized supply acquisition and capital. Out of funds, they shut down after running the pivot ~15 months.

## The decision without data (tech fest launch)
Six months in, the team kept polishing the product, scared to launch beyond friends. Arjun pushed to launch at NIT Warangal's tech fest — no data existed to support the call, so he reasoned through pros (real validation platform, direct user access, physical hand-holding, infra stress test, alma mater goodwill, sizeable audience) versus cons (bad first impression risk, infra crash risk, founder reputation). The blocker was actually fear — so he reframed the team's worry from "this will make us look bad" to "the real sin is making a bad product and not learning." They launched, and the fest learnings triggered the pivot away from pure planning. He tells this as his bias-for-action story.

## The failure and what it taught him
Arjun openly calls WhatsDplan his most instructive failure. He founded it on an unvalidated assumption, spent 8 months building before real validation, and his early research was designed to confirm his conviction rather than challenge it — surveys of friends biased toward building. He conflated "this is a common complaint" with "this is a problem acute enough to change behavior." When retention dropped post-launch he doubled down on features — the wrong diagnosis, because the need wasn't acute. His lasting rule: distinguish "people say this is annoying" from "people are actively doing something imperfect to solve it." If there's no workaround behavior, the pain won't pull people to a new tool. He now applies this lens to every feature request: how many users, how frequently, and what are they doing today to cope.
